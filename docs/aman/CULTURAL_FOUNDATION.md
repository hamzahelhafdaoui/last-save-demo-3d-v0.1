# AMAN — regional foundations and research ledger

This is the owner's alternate LAST SAVE direction, not a redesign of main. It is original fantasy rooted in North African environments and Amazigh everyday life. Playable in this checkpoint: an Atlas-inspired highland community, a southern Moroccan oasis, and a personal courtyard. Kabyle, Rif and Tuareg chapters remain distinct future work; they are not presented as completed places or interchangeable costumes.

The cedar highlands are a fictional composite of Moroccan Atlas landscapes, not an exact reconstruction of one named village. The oasis is a separate region. Climate and livelihoods explain the contrast. The story's water customs and granary decisions are fictional local institutions informed by research, not claims about universal or present-day Amazigh law.

## Language and names

Dialogue is English, consistently. Displayed Amazigh terms use Latin transcription with short English glosses. This is not a Tamazight translation/localization. No invented sentences, random Tifinagh ornament or unsupported personal-name etymologies are shipped.

- **Aman — water.** The title of the Encyclopédie berbère entry, *Aman “eaux”*, supports the gloss. [Entry reference](https://journals.openedition.org/encyclopedieberbere/2461). Its full page returned an anti-bot challenge during this session; the bibliographic title was independently indexed in the search results. This is not a claim to have read its full text.
- **Tizi — mountain pass.** Atlas toponymy is documented by Jeannine Drouin, *Éléments de toponymie berbère dans l'Atlas marocain*, 2003. [Persée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/onoma_0755-7752_2003_num_41_1_1451). “Tizi Highlands” is our fictional English/Amazigh region label, not a purported historic place name.
- **Targa — irrigation channel.** Documented in Georges Fay's *Sagesse ancienne, erreurs modernes dans la gestion des eaux de surface au Maroc*, 2000. [Persée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bagf_0004-5322_2000_num_77_1_2146). “Targa Oasis” is likewise fictional.
- **Itto, Hadda and Hammou** are fictional characters using attested personal names; no translations of these names are asserted. Itto is attested in an Atlas Tamazight author's own account ([Itto Outini](https://modernliterature.org/my-questions-my-answers-by-itto-mekiya-outini/)); Hadda Ouakki appears in an [IRCAM publication](https://biblio.ircam.ma/pmb/uploads/publications/143.pdf); Hammou Belghazi appears in [IRCAM's anthropological research](https://www.ircam.ma/fr/centre-des-etudes-anthropologiques-et-sociologiques). These NPCs do not depict those people. Precise local spelling, usage and character biographies still need speaker/community review.

The community NPC's spelling is **Hammou**; one consistent Latin form is used throughout the experience.

## Architecture, society and objects — evidence used

1. [M'Goun UNESCO Global Geopark: Aoujgal collective granaries](https://geoparcmgoun.ma/discover/geosite-21) — communal storage, cliff/stone construction, family cells and wooden locks. Informs the granary's many doors and the family-delivery quest; the building is an original composite, not a model of Aoujgal.
2. [UNESCO: Ksar of Ait-Ben-Haddou](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/444/) — southern Moroccan earthen settlement fabric and collective buildings. Informs the oasis's material, massing and fortified silhouettes; not copied as the Atlas village.
3. [UNESCO: knowledge and skills associated with khettaras](https://ich.unesco.org/en/assistances/knowledge-and-skills-associated-with-khettaras-02598) and [UNESCO water education](https://www.unesco.org/en/virtual-science-museum/voices-water/morocco) — groundwater galleries, surface delivery and maintenance. The game shows surface headworks and shafts; underground tunnels are not falsely offered as playable.
4. [IRCAM: Action collective en milieux amazighes](https://www.ircam.ma/fr/edition/action-collective-en-milieux-amazighes) and [community institutions research](https://www.ircam.ma/fr/actualites/marche-et-juridictions-eclairages-sur-la-transformation-institutions-communautaires-dans-les-milieux-amazighes) — a research foundation for institutions and collective action, rather than an assumption that a village is just scenery.
5. [Metropolitan Museum: Middle Atlas Zayan fibula](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/453172) — silver material and garment fastening. Its specific pear-shaped type is not treated as a universal design. Our simple silver clasp is original fantasy metalwork, not a replica assigned to all regions.
6. [Smithsonian: Art of Being Tuareg](https://www.si.edu/exhibitions/art-being-tuareg-sahara-nomads-modern-world-event-exhib-972) — distinct, evolving Tuareg arts across Mali, Niger and Algeria. Used to separate the future chapter's research scope, not to lift its objects into the Atlas/oasis wardrobe.
7. [IRCAM general dictionary](https://www.ircam.ma/fr/edition/le-dictionnaire-general-de-la-langue-amazighe) — future lexicon/localization reference; its description explicitly discusses a standardized lexicon without dialect provenance. It should not alone be used to claim a term is specific to a given region.

No external photographs, museum models, recordings or textile patterns were copied into the shipped assets. Original procedural geometry, materials and textile-style motifs are authored in `src/render/amazigh.ts`. The existing original fox traveler is retained as a fantasy protagonist, now wearing a tunic, woven belt, optional silver clasp and an obtainable oasis mantle.

## Fantasy and unfinished work

The Dawn Terrace, storykeeper, magic doorways, Dawnblade and optional trail shadows are original fantasy. They are not advertised as real Amazigh rituals, beliefs or sacred designs. The grain-choice quest has two humane outcomes and remembers the player's agreement; it does not turn a real cultural group into an enemy faction.

The score is an original synthesized plucked-string/soft-percussion sketch, with wind/water textures. It is not an authentic recording, a traditional repertoire, or a claim to reproduce one region's performance practice. Regional musicians and composers should shape the next audio pass.

There has been **no paid community consultation or native-speaker review** in this session. Further development should involve regional architects/artists, Amazigh speakers and musicians, with authorship, credit and compensation. Specific costume construction, gender/profession variation, food, seasonal celebrations, speech and social schedules need deeper work. Current NPCs have ambient movement and roles, not a full community simulation. Future regions remain locked until their own settings are designed and built.
